The Crumbling World

The sky had forgotten how to be blue. Grey ash drifted endlessly from clouds that looked like bruises against the sickly yellow horizon. Maya clutched her ration card with trembling fingers as she shuffled through the checkpoint queue. 

"Papers," the guard growled, his voice mechanical, devoid of humanity.

In the old world, they said people smiled at strangers. They said children played in parks without fear. Maya couldn't imagine such foolishness. Trust was a luxury that had died with the last green tree.

The marketplace was a graveyard of commerce. Vendors hawked their meager wares - dented cans of mystery meat, recycled water that tasted of rust and regret. Everything was scarce. Everything was rationed. Everything was controlled.

She passed the propaganda screens blaring their endless lies: "Unity through Compliance. Safety through Surveillance. Freedom through Obedience."

Nobody believed the words anymore, but nobody dared say so. The walls had ears. The neighbors had quotas. Betrayal was the only currency that never lost value.

Maya found her designated food station - Station 7, Sector B, Grid 42. Even hunger had been bureaucratized. Even starvation followed proper procedure.

This was life now. This was all there was. This was all there would ever be.

The ash continued to fall, like the tears of a world that had given up crying.
